Research scope: numerical investigation of a coupled gradient estimator for a
linear instrumental-variable problem. This task does NOT concern a trained
Transformer, in-context learning performance, or a proof of a general theorem.

Observed matrices are Z[n,q], X[n,p], Y[n], with n > q >= p. The centered model
has no intercept. Z is independent of latent U and observation errors. Generate
X = Z Theta_true + noise U Phi + E2 and
Y = X beta_true + noise U phi + e1.
Entries of Z, U, E2, e1 and parameter arrays Theta_true, beta_true, Phi, phi
are independent standard Gaussian draws before the shared-U terms are formed.
Sharing U permits endogeneity; it does not guarantee that every small sample
exhibits the same magnitude of OLS error. Baseline code supplies these draws.
Truth arrays may be used for evaluation only, not to initialize or fit a method.

Fixed method to implement:
  Theta_0 = zeros(q,p); b_0 = zeros(p).
  Theta_(t+1) = Theta_t - eta Z.T (Z Theta_t - X).
  b_(t+1) = b_t - alpha Theta_t.T Z.T (Z Theta_t b_t - Y).
Both right-hand sides use OLD states. In particular, do not use Theta_(t+1)
in the b update. Gradients are unnormalized sums: there is no hidden 1/n or 2
factor. If using an equivalent normalized implementation, document the exact
step-size conversion. alpha and eta are positive and constant within a run.
Do not replace either iterative state by an exact least-squares fit during the
run. Exact fits are legitimate comparators and step-size diagnostics.

Useful definitions, not empirical conclusions:
  Theta_hat = argmin_T ||Z T - X||_F^2.
  b_2sls = argmin_b ||Z Theta_hat b - Y||_2^2.
  A = (Z Theta_hat).T (Z Theta_hat); B = Z.T Z.
  gamma(alpha) = spectral_radius(I_p - alpha A).
  kappa(eta) = spectral_radius(I_q - eta B).
  Lambda = max(gamma, kappa).
The candidate stable region is 0 < alpha < 2/sigma_max(Z Theta_hat)^2,
0 < eta < 2/sigma_max(Z)^2, with full-rank Z and Z Theta_hat. These are
hypothesis conditions to investigate, not a guarantee that each finite trace
must decrease monotonically or that a finite simulation proves a theorem.

Hypotheses to address in full:
H1. For the supplied identified Gaussian regime and steps within the stated
region, the coupled iteration approaches the sample 2SLS solution; its late
optimization error is compatible with a geometric-rate description involving
Lambda. Distinguish finite transients, conditioning and numerical precision
from asymptotic behavior. Report unsupported cases rather than force a fit.
H2. Changing the two step sizes changes the bottleneck and finite-iteration
behavior; the simple Lambda diagnostic is informative about which block limits
progress. Its limitations and behavior outside the candidate region should be
investigated, without asserting that every outside-region setting must diverge.

Design your own informative settings, repetitions, diagnostics, comparisons and
uncertainty analysis. The supplied defaults n=50,p=5,q=10,noise=1 are a starting
point, not a mandatory full experimental design. You may vary dimensions with
n>q>=p, noise>=0, random seeds and positive steps, and extend iteration length
within resources. Keep both hypotheses and the fixed algorithm semantics.
Do not infer Transformer capabilities, population consistency, or universal
causal identification from this finite numerical task. The target is sample
2SLS, not the latent true coefficient; those errors answer different questions.
